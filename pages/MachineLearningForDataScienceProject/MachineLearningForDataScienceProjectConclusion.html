Conclusion

Following the completion of this project, there are two primary ideas for future work that could be performed to get novel results:

Creating an ensemble model with special sub-models may lead to greatly improved performance. The original EPI dataset is broken into "policy objective" categories. Creating a model for each of these categories, then producing a combination of them, could lead to greater overall predictive power in addition to the improved granularity of predictions.

Looking at the original time series version of the EPI data may result in new insights. Having explored a recent snapshot of the pollution data, it may be worthwhile in the future to re-structure the dataset to produce a new system that could be analyzed over time. This may yield new results, but there is a high likelihood that it would simply show that pollution is on the rise.

This project was, overall, an unconventional success. The results of the supervised learning models proved to be an interesting case to analyze, which offered personal learning experience. The generally good performance of those models also led to one subtle question being answered, in the end: Could the ND-GAIN Index be approximated without having or even knowing the original factors used to compute it? The answer seems to be yes; this is notable for enabling the score to be approximated by the models shown here without needing to rely on the original data used to compute it. In the event that some data was missing, and that a prediction would be useful before that data was collected, these models would be useful to determine whether a country was at high risk or not.
